\title{Low-Cost Video--Time Priors Improve EEG--fNIRS Emotion Regression for New Viewers of Familiar Videos}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\affiliation{%
  \institution{Anonymous Institution}
  \country{}
}
\email{}

\begin{abstract}
Continuous emotion regression can estimate how viewers' valence and arousal
change while they watch a known video. Low-error trajectories may provide a
group-response baseline for video editing, advertising placement, content
recommendation, or coarse initialization for a new viewer. We therefore study
the practical objective of reducing mean absolute error (MAE) for new viewers
of familiar, time-aligned videos. Our predictor constructs a fold-wise
video--time prior from training viewers and combines it with an EEG--fNIRS
branch through fixed fusion. Across 24 participants viewing 15 shared videos,
fixed fusion achieved the lowest MAE of 29.0146; the low-cost prior and
physiological branch reached 29.0633 and 47.3509, respectively. In an external
subject-disjoint evaluation on four viewers and 6,143 one-second samples, fixed
fusion again achieved the lowest MAE of 27.7246. Post-hoc comparisons yielded
44.3296, 33.3627, 28.0410, and 42.7486 for a global constant, video identity,
video--time prior, and physiology, respectively. Thus, the video--time prior is itself a
simple and effective predictor in this deployment scope, while physiology
provides a smaller additional reduction. After establishing the MAE result, we
use source-explicit ablations and paired residuals to show that the gain is
mainly associated with video identity and within-video time, and that the
fusion increment occurs in 3 of 4 viewers and 9 of 15 videos. The method targets
new-viewer prediction for familiar videos and does not establish transfer to
unseen content. Optimizing MAE first and analysing its sources afterwards
provides both a useful prediction strategy and an interpretable scope of use.
\end{abstract}
