\title{Shared-Stimulus Structure Dominates Subject-Held-Out EEG--fNIRS Emotion Regression}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\affiliation{%

  \country{}

\email{}

\begin{abstract}
Continuous emotion regression seeks to estimate moment-to-moment valence and
arousal from physiological recordings. When every participant views the same
time-aligned videos, however, subject-held-out evaluation leaves stimulus
identity and time unchanged. This creates a source ambiguity: apparent
prediction accuracy may reflect a stimulus-locked group trajectory rather than
information decoded from an individual's physiology. We introduce a
source-explicit analysis that separates a fold-wise video--time prior from an
EEG--fNIRS predictor. The prior is constructed only from training participants,
whereas the physiological branch uses baseline-centred spectral, statistical,
graph, and cross-modal representations. Across 24 participants viewing 15
shared videos, the physiological branch obtained a mean absolute error (MAE) of
47.3509, the prior 29.0633, and their fixed fusion 29.0146. Thus, under this
repeated-stimulus protocol, most predictive performance is attributable to
shared stimulus structure and the measured physiological increment is small.
This result does not imply that EEG or fNIRS lacks affective information; it
shows that subject-held-out evaluation alone cannot identify its contribution
when stimuli repeat. Source-explicit baselines and crossed subject-by-stimulus
splits are therefore necessary for interpretable emotion regression.
\end{abstract}
